About Autumn Root Journal

Independent, reader-supported, and rooted in slow gardening.

Autumn Root Journal began in 2019 as a printed zine for a small circle of allotment gardeners in the Pacific Northwest. Today it’s a digital publication reaching over 40,000 growers each month — but we still write every article by hand, from experience.

We believe that gardening is less about quick fixes and more about observation, patience, and a deep respect for soil life. Our articles avoid hype and focus on methods that work in real gardens, across climates and seasons.

Our editorial ethos

Every piece we publish is grounded in practice. We test techniques in our own gardens, talk to experienced growers, and cite soil science when it matters. No listicles, no fluff, no AI-generated content. We write for the gardener who wants to understand why something works, not just how.

We are entirely ad-free. Our only revenue comes from voluntary reader donations and occasional workshops. This keeps our content independent and free from brand influence. When we recommend a tool or a variety, it’s because we use it ourselves — never because someone paid us.

Who we are

Elena Marsh is the founding editor. She has been growing food for 22 years and holds a certificate in soil science from Oregon State University. She writes most of the articles and edits every contribution. Her garden is a half-acre of clay loam, cover crops, and experimental compost piles in the Willamette Valley.

From time to time, we welcome guest articles from experienced growers who share our values. If you’d like to pitch, please read our contact page for guidelines.

What you’ll find here

We publish in-depth articles on soil health, composting, seasonal planting, seed saving, tool care, and the quiet craft of growing food. Whether you’re a balcony gardener or managing a small farm, our aim is to offer thoughtful, practical wisdom that helps you grow more resilient gardens — and more patient gardeners.

Thank you for reading. We’re glad you’re here.

— Elena Marsh, editor
